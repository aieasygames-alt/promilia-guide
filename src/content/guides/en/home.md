## Build one useful production chain first

**Start with an order you can work toward, then trace its ingredients, processing stations and Kibo jobs. Check knowledge unlocks for a locked recipe, prepare farmland before sowing, and reduce optional jobs if food keeps falling.** Keeping one useful chain running gives you a clearer next step than filling every assignment immediately.

| What is stopping you? | What to check and do |
| --- | --- |
| A workbench recipe is locked | Its unlock shortcut and knowledge menu. Requirements, available points and materials |
| Seeds are stocked but nothing grows | Prepared farmland and seed storage. Sowing, watering and harvesting assignments |
| Workers are assigned but production stops | The station’s displayed reason. Ingredients, job suitability, food and storage |
| You do not know what to make | Current order requirements. Processing steps and market information |
| A full workforce runs short of food | Jobs needed for your current output. Dining-table supply and changing food stock |

The route below draws on [YaMou’s illustrated November 26 community guide](https://forum.gamer.com.tw/C.php?bsn=79865&snA=696) in Traditional Chinese. Original labels are retained for comparison; English glosses are descriptive, not confirmed English-client names. The author’s PC shortcuts may differ by edition, controller layout or custom bindings. China and Korean menu references appear separately below.

## First home setup: recipes, knowledge, fields and seeds

### Check knowledge before gathering for a locked recipe

YaMou starts at the workbench and uses a **0/1** marker to identify an item not yet crafted. Missing ingredients send you back to their gathering sources; a locked item instead calls for its unlock shortcut, **前往查看**, or the **心得** knowledge menu. The post points to industrial and farming-related entries there.

Prioritize an unlock that addresses your immediate shortage. If an order needs a processed item, find that item’s recipe first. If farming cannot run, inspect the relevant farming requirements. A missing ingredient and a locked recipe need different solutions. The author encourages spending knowledge points; this guide does not turn that advice into a universal point-allocation order or a reason to use every material just to clear a marker.

### Prepare the field before adding workers

The post warns that it is easy to overlook preparing farmland after a home upgrade. On the author’s PC setup, **E** is used to till the ground before arranging work through **耕作小屋**, the farming hut. If you already have seeds and workers, inspect the field before capturing more Kibo.

Check these separately:

1. Is the intended farmland prepared?
2. Are the seeds stocked and arranged in **種子倉庫**, the seed warehouse?
3. Do suitable Kibo cover sowing, watering and harvesting?

YaMou lists wheat, corn and carrots as early crops and describes automatic planting and harvesting with the jobs covered. This is the author’s account of their edition, not a timing or automation test by this site. Use your station display to check unlocked crops, continued operation and collection. A seed queue alone does not establish that ingredients and food will last.

### Put frequently used manual stations within easy reach

The author keeps the workbench, furnace, cooking pot and alchemy station nearby because they require frequent manual visits. The order board and some resource buildings can instead be operated or collected through **G → 家園中樞**, the home hub, in the author’s setup.

Use that as a layout principle: inspect what your own hub can operate, then keep the stations you actually walk to close together. It is not a fixed-coordinate layout or a measured maximum-efficiency design. On mobile or a controller, follow the current client’s menu rather than the PC key label.

## Work backward from an order to its ingredients

YaMou’s route is **craft 交易站 at the workbench → read the order board → check 行商日記 for current market information → check 明日預告 for the next-day preview**. These original labels mean a trading station, merchant diary and tomorrow preview. Complete a manageable current order before committing stock to a later market opportunity.

| Step | What to check and do |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the requested product | Raw material, intermediate or finished product, plus quantity. Keep the requested item separate from similar ingredients |
| Find its processing step | Workbench or furnace recipe and lock status. Check knowledge for a lock; sources for missing inputs |
| Assign the necessary work | Ingredients, jobs and food supply. Feed the useful chain before optional production |
| Decide what surplus to sell | Current listed prices and your next material needs. Reserve progression materials before selling leftovers |

The author recommends processing goods before selling and looking for higher-priced intermediate or finished products. **A higher sale price is not automatically a higher net return.** The sequence in the table is this site’s planning advice: consider processing inputs and your own needs as well as the listed sale price. The available evidence does not establish one most-profitable product for every account.

The next-day preview can help you decide what to inspect on your next visit; it is not a reason to put all stock into one product. The post also points to **栗花小舖** as a place to inspect what can be made at the current level. Compare it with the workbench and your orders rather than assuming a complete recipe list here.

## Home currency: compare weekly limits with permanent purchases

The post calls the currency **閃亮的硬幣** and points to **閃亮奇珍** for weekly and permanently limited purchases. YaMou prioritizes **原初水滴**, described as restoring stamina. This is a Traditional Chinese community shopping recommendation, not a verified shop table for China, Japan or a future English edition.

Before spending, read the price, remaining purchases, reset notice and item effect in your own shop. With limited currency, compare an offer that resets with a permanent purchase you could make later, then decide whether you need the stamina item. Do not infer another region’s stock, prices or weekly reset time from this post.

China’s official benefit images separately list eight ordinary tickets from home-level conditions and eight through the home shop. They have separate requirements, not 16 tickets immediately on entering the home. See [regional launch rewards](/en/guides/resources/) when comparing progression rewards with purchases.

## Link exploration to the material you need

YaMou recommends opening map nodes and interacting with outdoor facilities such as mining sites, logging sites and elemental altars along the way. The post connects these with home resource output and some crop unlocks. If an order introduces a material you cannot produce, check for a relevant unopened outdoor facility as well as your home assignments.

A useful trip can follow this sequence: **read the shortage → inspect the material source → open relevant travel nodes and facilities on the way → return to check available production → update seeds and processing**. This is an editorial route through the tasks, not a full map route. It does not supply verified coordinates, unlock levels or output multipliers. Use the [Kibo guide](/en/systems/kibo/) when matching companion capabilities to a job.

## China and Korean seed-management references

### China farm menus

In a [November 26 TapTap post](https://www.taptap.cn/moment/875088587471717682), **雅中林阁** describes a first-day level 1–15 plan with **耕作小屋** (farming hut), **奇波餐桌** (Kibo dining table), **订单看板** (order board) and **奇波牧场** (Kibo ranch). The author says they did not play the beta, and comments correct parts of the post. Its title is not a verified facility unlock-level table.

- Read **优先播种高星种子** before enabling higher-star seed priority; make sure the selected crops still match your current order.
- Check the dining table before using **一键添加**, the one-action food addition. Read which food it will add.
- Match the displayed sowing, watering and harvesting abilities to the jobs. Rarity alone does not show suitability.

### Korean seed-management path

**Horol’s** [November 27 post](https://arca.live/b/azurpromilia/190507810) shows **home overview → 키보 농장 → bottom-right 씨앗 관리 → 씨앗 창고 and the seed slots**. The author describes planting in the configured order. Discovering it at level 21 does not prove it unlocks at level 21; their 15–20 minute manual wheat-planting anecdote is not a crop timer. Check seed configuration and output collection separately.

## Why does a full workforce run short of food?

A [China player report read November 27](https://www.taptap.cn/moment/875286112719866850) describes food shortages with filled jobs and questions whether acceleration or refund passives repay the added consumption. It does not provide a controlled configuration, universal hourly rate or evidence that every high-rarity trait is bad.

Keep the jobs needed for the current order and temporarily reduce optional work. Check the dining table and food stock, then add optional jobs gradually once production recovers. Account for manual feeding, reward income and order hand-ins when watching the inventory. This helps locate the shortage without replacing the whole workforce at once.

If a station still stops, read its message and check **seeds or ingredients → job suitability → food → timer → storage and collection** before expanding further.

## Japan housing is separate from production unlocks

Japan’s [launch announcement](https://azurpromilia.jp/news/5225) lists farm housing, **寮**, for character interactions. 塗山小玉’s **春還る花亭** is listed until **January 5, 2027, 04:59 JST**; メーツァ’s **翠緑の秘苑** is a permanent sale. Decorations, pajamas and avatar items do not establish a production bonus or mandatory farming purchase.

If the home is still locked, follow your current quest and its requirements through [the beginner guide](/en/guides/getting-started/). Once it is open, finish one chain from demand to ingredients, processing and delivery, then expand.
